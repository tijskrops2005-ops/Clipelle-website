Your Shopify product page already has what a short ad needs: the product name, a description and photos. Retyping all of that into another form is the kind of small friction that stops people making ads for more than their one best seller. Clipelle's [URL to video](/platform/url-to-video) removes it. You paste the link to a product page you own, the name, description and photos are read for you, you check them, and the normal ad form opens prefilled. This article explains what gets read, what you should check, and how to make ads for a whole store look like they belong to one store.

## What happens when you paste a link

Clipelle reads the public product page on the server and fills in three things: the product name, the description and the large photos it finds. Nothing is created at that point. You see the name and description as editable text and the photos as a set you can tick or leave unticked. Only after you confirm are the photos you ticked copied, and only then does the ad form open with everything in place. From there the flow is the same as starting from a photo: pick a length, a presenter, a tone, and click Generate. If you have not seen that flow, [from one product photo to a finished ad](/blog/from-one-product-photo-to-a-finished-ad) walks through it.

Two rules before you paste anything. The page has to be public, because a draft or password-protected page cannot be read. And it has to be your product and your photos. Pasting someone else's product page is not allowed, and the rights confirmation on the form is there for a reason.

## Check the name and the description

The name comes through as it is on the page. If your product title includes a variant or a search phrase, such as "Linen Shirt - Sand - Men's Relaxed Fit Summer Shirt," trim it to what the presenter should say out loud: "Linen Shirt." The script will mention the name, and a long title spoken aloud sounds like a catalog.

The description deserves more attention, because the script is written from it. A Shopify description is written to be read: materials, dimensions, care, shipping. A script is written to be spoken, in the shape hook, problem, product, benefits, call to action, within a hard limit of about 2.5 words per second. At 30 seconds that is 75 words. The automatic script does the turning, but it can only work with what is in front of it. Before you confirm, make sure the first two or three sentences of the description say who the product is for and what problem it solves. If your page description starts with "Dimensions: 12 × 8 × 3 cm," move that down and put the point of the product first. The guide [a product description that makes a good script](/resources/guides/product-description-that-makes-a-good-script) goes into this in more detail.

## Tick the right photo

You get one product clip per ad, made from one photo with slow camera movement. Tick the photo where the product is large, sharp and alone. On many product pages the first image is a lifestyle shot with the product small in a scene, and the second or third is the clean packshot; the packshot is usually the better choice for the clip. Avoid photos with text or badges on them, since the only text that belongs on screen is the captions.

If the page has several variants, tick the photo of the variant you want the ad to be about. One ad, one variant. An ad for the other colors is the same description with a different photo ticked.

## A worked example

Say you sell a ceramic pour-over coffee dripper. You paste the product page link. The name comes back as "Pour-Over Dripper, Stoneware, Matte White"; you shorten it to "Pour-Over Dripper." The description comes back with two paragraphs about the stoneware and a paragraph of dimensions; you keep the first two and cut the dimensions. You tick the packshot on the plain background and leave the two lifestyle photos unticked. You confirm, choose 30 seconds, Standard quality, the Direct tone, and turn on "Review script first." A few moments later you get three hooks and a script along these lines, 56 words:

> Good coffee at home should not need a machine. Most drippers are plastic, and the water cools before it hits the grounds. This one is thick ceramic that holds heat while you pour. It fits any standard filter, sits on your own mug, and rinses clean in seconds. Find it in the shop at the link.

You pick the hook, change "the shop" to your store name, and let it run. The ad is ready in minutes; you can go back to your admin while it renders and the project page shows each step as it completes.

## Make the whole store look like one store

A store with twenty ads needs them to match. Clipelle keeps the visual choices small and lets you save them once in Settings as brand defaults:

- The accent color, which highlights the word being spoken in the captions. Use your store's primary color.
- The caption look and the font.
- Your logo, which sits small in the top right.

With those saved, every ad you start from a product page comes out in the same style without you touching a setting. The presenter background can be a flat color from your palette or your own image, so even the backdrop can match the store.

Pick one presenter and one voice for the store and keep them. Customers who see three of your ads should feel they are hearing from the same shop. The tone can vary by product line if you want: Direct for tools and basics, Warm for home goods, Elegant for gifts, Playful for anything aimed at kids.

## Offers and the call to action

The call to action is spoken by the presenter, not shown as a button. If you run a discount or free shipping, put it in the Offer line on the form, at most 80 characters. It is the only way a deal gets into the spoken call to action. Keep it true for as long as the ad runs, and when the offer ends, make a new version without it rather than leaving an ad that promises something your checkout does not deliver.

## Where the finished ad goes

Download the 9:16 MP4 and post it wherever your customers are: TikTok, Reels and Shorts are the formats it is made for. Many Shopify themes also show a video in the product gallery, and a 30-second spoken explanation next to the photos is a natural fit there, with the captions doing the work for people browsing with the sound off.

## Doing it for many products

If you want an ad for every product in a collection, the form is still the fastest way for the first few, because it makes you check the name, description and photo per product, and that check is where the quality comes from. For larger numbers, Clipelle has an API and an MCP server, so an assistant or a script can create ads from product links and read back their status. The same checks still apply: the products must be yours, and someone should still read the first script of each new product line.

## A short checklist per product page

1. The page is public and the product is yours.
2. Name trimmed to what should be spoken.
3. Description starts with who it is for and what it solves; specifications moved down or cut.
4. One clean packshot ticked, no text on it.
5. One variant per ad.
6. Offer line only for a deal you will honor.
7. Brand defaults saved once.
8. "Review script first" on for the first product of each line.
