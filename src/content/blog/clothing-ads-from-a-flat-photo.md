Clothing is the category where "we need a model and a shoot" feels most true. The garment has to be seen on a body, the fabric has to move, the fit has to be shown. All of that is right for a catalog. For a short vertical ad it is less true than it feels. Most clothing shops already have the photo that works: a flat lay, a ghost mannequin shot or a clean hanger photo. This article is about turning that photo into an ad with [Clipelle](/platform/ads), and about what to say when nobody in the picture is wearing the clothes.

## What you get, and what you do not

Set expectations first. Clipelle makes one product clip from your photo with slow camera movement, lays it over an AI presenter who speaks the script, and adds captions of what is said. The clip does not invent a model and put your shirt on them. A flat lay stays a flat lay, in motion. A mannequin stays a mannequin.

That is fine, because the human side of the ad is the presenter. The presenter talks about the garment while it appears on screen at fixed moments. The viewer hears why it matters and sees what it looks like. For a 30-second ad that is exactly the split you want: words carry the fit, the feel and the occasion; the picture carries the cut, the color and the print.

## Which photo to pick

You get one product clip per ad, so pick the one photo that sells the piece. Three kinds of photo come up most, and each suits a different garment. For general advice on what makes a photo work in motion, read [product photos that make a good product clip](/blog/product-photos-that-make-a-good-product-clip); the points below are specific to clothing.

### The flat lay

A garment laid out and photographed from above. It shows the cut, the print and the proportions clearly. Pick it when the pattern or the color is the reason people buy: a printed shirt, a graphic tee, a scarf, kids' clothes. Lay it on a plain background with no props that compete. Fold the sleeves so the shape reads at a glance.

### The ghost mannequin

The garment photographed on an invisible form, so it has volume but no body. It shows how the piece hangs, where the waist sits, how a collar stands. Pick it when fit and drape are the selling point: jackets, dresses, tailored trousers, knitwear. A slow camera move over a mannequin shot gives the viewer the sense of looking around the piece.

### The on-model photo you own

If you have a photo of a person wearing the garment and you own the rights to it, you can use it. The clip adds movement to the photo; the person, their face and the outfit stay as they are. This is the right choice when the garment only makes sense on a body, such as swimwear or activewear. It is the wrong choice when the photo is a small figure in a large scene, because the garment will be small on a phone screen.

### The detail shot

A close-up of fabric, stitching or a button. Beautiful, but on its own it does not tell the viewer what the piece is. Use it only when the detail is the whole point and the name says the rest, as with a cashmere scarf. Otherwise pick one of the three above and let the presenter mention the detail.

## The script: fabric, fit, occasion

Clipelle writes the script automatically from your product name and two or three sentences, in the shape hook, problem, product, benefits, call to action, with a hard limit of about 2.5 words per second. At 30 seconds that is 75 words. Clothing scripts work best when those sentences cover three things the photo cannot show:

- **Fabric.** What it feels like, how heavy it is, how it behaves after washing.
- **Fit.** Relaxed, slim, true to size, runs large. Say it plainly; it saves returns.
- **Occasion.** When the viewer would wear it. The occasion is often the real hook.

Here is a 30-second script for a linen shirt, 71 words, that you could write yourself or get from the automatic script with a description that covers those three points:

> Linen that doesn't look slept in by noon. Most linen shirts crease the moment you sit down, and ironing one every morning is not a life. This one is cut from a heavier weave with a relaxed fit. It holds its shape through a full day, breathes when it's hot, and goes straight from the wash to the hanger. Pick your size at the link, with free returns for thirty days.

Notice what is not in it: no list of colors, no size chart, no care instructions. Those belong on the product page. The ad has one job, which is to make the viewer want to see the product page.

If you want to see the three hooks and edit the words before anything is rendered, turn on "Review script first." It pauses after the script and lets you change the wording before the presenter and the clip are made.

## Sizes, colors and variants

One ad is about one garment in one color. If the piece comes in six colors, pick the hero color for the photo and let the presenter say "in six colors" if that matters. If you want an ad per color, that is six projects with the same description and a different photo; the words can stay the same.

Do not put the size range in the script unless an unusual range is a selling point ("sizes XS to 4XL" can be). Do put the fit in. "Runs a size large" spoken out loud prevents more returns than a size chart nobody reads.

The deal goes in the Offer line, at most 80 characters, and it is the only way a discount or free returns gets into the spoken call to action. "Free returns for thirty days" is 28 characters. Keep it true; the presenter will say it.

## Tone and presenter

Clipelle has four tones. A rough match for clothing:

- **Elegant** for occasion wear, tailoring, silk, cashmere.
- **Playful** for kids' clothes, streetwear, prints.
- **Direct** for basics and essentials, where the point is fabric and price.
- **Warm** for loungewear, knitwear, anything you buy to feel at home in.

Pick a presenter whose look fits the shop, not the garment. The presenter is not modeling the piece; they are your shop talking. A language, accent and voice choice follows from where your customers are. Standard presenter quality is recommended; Premium adds more natural head and hand movement and the best lip-sync at a higher cost per second, which matters more for close-up talking than for a clothing ad where the garment takes the screen at fixed moments.

## Brand style

Clothing brands live on a consistent look, and the few visual choices in Clipelle are made for that. The accent color highlights the spoken word in the captions; set it to a color from your palette. Pick a caption look and a font once and save them as brand defaults in Settings so every ad for the shop matches. The small logo top right is optional. The presenter's background can be a flat color from your palette or your own image, such as the shop interior or a textured wall that matches the season's photos.

Nothing else appears on screen: no text cards, no end card, no price sticker. The captions are the only text, which keeps the garment and the presenter in view.

## A checklist before you click Generate

- One garment, one color, one photo with the piece large and clear.
- Description covers fabric, fit and occasion in two or three sentences.
- Length chosen (30 seconds is the default and a good start).
- Offer line filled in only if you will honor it.
- Tone matched to the garment.
- Brand defaults set: accent color, caption look, font, logo.
- Estimate on the button checked.
- "Review script first" on for the first ad of a new line, so you can read the words before anything is made.
