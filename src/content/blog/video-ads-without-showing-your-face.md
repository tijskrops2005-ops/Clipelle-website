Many shop owners never put their face in a video. Some do not like how they look on camera, some sell under a brand name they want to keep separate from their own, and some simply do not have the time to record take after take. None of that stops you from running video ads. There are two workable ways to make a video ad without appearing in it yourself, and both come with trade-offs worth knowing before you pick one.

## Why the face matters at all

Short vertical ads work because they feel like a person talking to another person. A face gives the viewer something to lock onto in the first second, and a voice carries the pitch while the thumb hovers over the screen. Take the face away completely and you need something else to do that job: strong product footage, a clear voice, and captions that are easy to read.

So the question is not "face or no face." It is "whose face, or what instead."

## Route one: product only, your voice or none

The first route is to keep yourself out of the frame and show only the product. You film with your phone: hands unboxing, the product being used, close-ups of the details. Then you record a voiceover or let captions carry the words.

What works well here:

- Products that are interesting to watch: things that move, pour, fold, light up or change.
- Demonstrations where the hands tell the story (applying, assembling, tapping).
- Brands that want a quiet, premium feel with no personality attached.

Where it gets hard:

- You still need footage. A product that sits still on a table gives you ten seconds of material, not thirty.
- A voiceover recorded in a kitchen sounds like a voiceover recorded in a kitchen. Room echo and background noise are the first things people notice.
- Without a person on screen, the hook has to come from the words or from motion. That puts more pressure on the first sentence.

If you have the time and a product that films well, this is a good route. Keep the clips short, cut on the beat of the sentence, and make sure the captions are large enough to read on a phone.

## Route two: an AI presenter speaks for you

The second route is to put a face in the video, just not yours. An AI presenter is a realistic, generated person who speaks a script you provide or approve. You choose the look, the voice and the language. The presenter does the talking; your product appears in a clip laid over the presenter at the right moments.

This is what Clipelle does. You upload one product photo and write two or three sentences about the product. A spoken script is written for you (hook, problem, product, benefits, call to action), a product clip with slow camera moves is generated from the photo, the presenter speaks the script, and the final edit places the product clip over the presenter with short fades and captions over the whole video. You never record anything.

What this route gives you:

- A face and a voice without filming. The viewer gets the "person talking to me" feel.
- Consistency. The same presenter can front every ad in a series, so the ads look like they belong together.
- Speed. A typical ad is ready in minutes, and you can leave the page while it renders.
- Languages. The same product can be presented in several languages with a matching voice and accent, without hiring a speaker per market.

What it does not give you:

- Your own personality. If customers buy from you because they know you, an AI presenter is a different kind of ad, not a replacement for you.
- Hands on the product. The presenter does not hold or demonstrate the product. The product clip does that job, and it is generated from a photo, so it shows the product with camera movement rather than someone using it.

## Choosing between the two

A short way to decide:

| Your situation | Better fit |
|---|---|
| The product is visually active and you have time to film | Product only |
| You have one good photo and no footage | AI presenter |
| You want a series of ads that look consistent | AI presenter |
| Your brand voice is deliberately impersonal | Product only |
| You sell in more than one language | AI presenter |

You can also mix them over time: start with presenter ads to test which message lands, then film a product-only version of the winner once you know what to say.

## Making a presenter ad feel right

An AI presenter is only as good as the choices around it. A few things that matter:

**Pick a presenter that fits the product, not the one that looks most striking.** A calm, neutral look suits most products. Clipelle offers portrait looks in two qualities: Standard, which is recommended for most ads, and Premium, with more natural head and hand movement and the best lip-sync, at a higher cost per second. The article on [choosing a presenter and a voice](/blog/choosing-a-presenter-and-voice-for-your-ad) goes deeper.

**Match the voice to the audience.** Language, accent and speaking speed are separate choices. A slightly slower pace helps when the product needs explaining; a faster pace suits a simple offer.

**Let the words sound like speech.** The script is spoken, so short sentences beat clever ones. If you want control, turn on "Review script first": the run pauses after the script, you pick one of three hooks and edit the words before anything else is generated. The word limit is about 2.5 words per second, so a 30-second ad has room for about 75 words.

**Keep the screen clean.** In a Clipelle ad, the only text on screen is the captions of what the presenter says, plus an optional small logo in the top right corner. That is deliberate. Captions are what people read with the sound off; extra text cards would compete with them.

## Being open about it

If you use an AI presenter, be clear about it where a platform or a law asks for it, and never present the presenter as a real customer giving a testimonial. The presenter is a spokesperson, not a reviewer. Say true things about the product and let the presenter say them well. The article on [UGC-style ads](/blog/ugc-style-ads-what-makes-them-feel-real) covers where the line between "feels real" and "pretends to be real" sits.

## A sample script for a faceless-by-choice brand

Here is a 30-second script of about 70 words, written for a presenter ad for a ceramic pour-over coffee dripper:

> Still drinking coffee that tastes flat by the second cup? Most drippers pour too fast and pull out the bitter notes. This ceramic dripper slows the flow, so every cup tastes like the first. It holds heat, fits any mug, and cleans in seconds. Order yours today and taste the difference tomorrow morning.

The product clip shows the dripper on a wooden counter with a slow push-in; the presenter carries the rest. Nobody from the company appears, and nobody needs to.
